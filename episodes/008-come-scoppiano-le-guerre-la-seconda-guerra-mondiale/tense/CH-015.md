## 15. Delayed Declarations and Fascist Propaganda

<!-- transcript: U-01096–U-01117; omissions: none -->

Two more days pass before Britain and France declare war on Germany, in the mad and naïve but sincere hope that this catastrophe can still be avoided. The interval includes ultimatums and continued diplomatic exchanges: Britain’s ultimatum expires at eleven in the morning on 3 September, and France’s at five that afternoon. [C-041]

Meanwhile, Mussolini is calm in Italy. Hitler has accepted that Italy will remain outside, and so they wait and see whether, as Ciano says, they too can obtain a share of the spoils. The Fascist government’s chosen status is non-belligerency, not neutrality, and the calculation is conditional rather than a settled annexation program. Ciano had recorded the idea on 13 and 15 August: “13 August: 'The Duce's reactions are varied. At first he agrees with me. Then he says that honor obliges him to march with Germany. Finally he states that he wants his share of the spoils in Croatia and Dalmatia.' 15 August: 'For in his view it is still possible—though difficult—that the democracies will back down. In that case it would not suit us to have clashed with Germany, since we too must take our share of the spoils. We must therefore find a solution that allows us: (a) if the democracies attack, to disengage ourselves “honorably” from the Germans; (b) if the democracies swallow it, to seize the opportunity to settle accounts with Belgrade once and for all.'” [Q-046] [C-042]

Naturally, the Italian press is given its instructions. It does not say any of this; it has to say something else. So I will close with the *Corriere della Sera* of 1 September: “THE DECISIVE HOUR STRIKES / Hitler's proposals for Danzig and the Corridor—loyal, reasonable, and eminently feasible—foolishly allowed to lapse by Warsaw and London / England and company nailed to their tremendous responsibilities / The publication of this page of history confirms Germany's conscious wisdom and moderation in the face of the blind intransigence of the other parties to the conflict” [Q-047]. These are the newspapers our fathers read in this happy country of ours in 1939.

Thank you.
