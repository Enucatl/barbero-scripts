## 9. Ciano learns Germany wants war

<!-- transcript: U-00561–U-00595; omissions: none -->

In his diary entry of 4 August, Ciano writes: “I am beginning to consider the advisability of meeting Ribbentrop. The time has come to find out what the situation really is. The stakes are too high for us to wait passively for developments.” [Q-022]

So Ciano flies to Germany. On 11 August he meets his counterpart, German foreign minister Joachim von Ribbentrop. Ciano has been sent to explain that starting a war at that moment would be madness and that it absolutely must not be done. In a later recollection, Ciano describes asking Ribbentrop as they walk together in the garden: “‘Well, Ribbentrop,’ I asked him as we walked side by side in the garden, ‘what do you want? The Corridor or Danzig?’ ‘Not anymore,’ and he fixed me with those cold Musée Grévin eyes of his, ‘we want war.’” [Q-023]

They are at a reception, all standing there with champagne glasses in hand. Ciano is burning to tell his staff what Ribbentrop has just told him, but they are still at the reception. Ciano later writes all this down. At last he passes close to one of his aides and whispers in his ear, “We’re headed for a fight.”

Ciano speaks further with Ribbentrop. The message is that war with Poland has been decided, and Poland will be crushed. And Britain? France? Ribbentrop believes they would not intervene. And then there is the wager, as in 1914, for those of you who were here last night. The wager is that they will not intervene. Except this time—you know how history repeats itself, first as tragedy and then as farce?—they really make a bet.

In Ciano’s later account: “They were making a fundamental error of calculation: they were certain that France and England would stand idly by while Poland was slaughtered. On this subject Ribbentrop wanted, during one of those gloomy meals we took at the Oesterreichischer Hof in Salzburg, to make a wager with me. I was to give him an Italian painting in the event of Anglo-French neutrality; he was to give me a collection of antique weapons in the event of intervention. Many witnesses were present, and not very long ago we were speaking about it with Ambassador von Mackensen. Ribbentrop, however, preferred to forget that wager and never paid his debt.” [Q-024] The point is that Germany will go to war without producing a world war, because France and Britain will not dare intervene. [C-024]

The gala dinner ends, then the reception, the toasts, everything. Ciano returns to the hotel with his aides, burning to tell them everything. Once there, he begins to wonder whether the Germans might have planted microphones in the room. So they lock themselves in the bathroom, and there Ciano tells his aides that the Germans have now decided on war. And here the countdown begins: 12 August.
