## 3. Anschluss: engineered crisis and non-response

<!-- transcript: U-00182–U-00211; omissions: none -->

And so in Austria there is a Nazi party which wants annexation to Germany and takes orders from Hitler. At a certain point Hitler gives the Nazis in Austria the go-ahead: create agitation, attacks, terrorism, disorder, political murders. [C-006]

Finally, at a certain point, it is March 1938. Four months have passed since Lord Halifax’s visit to Berlin, when he told Hitler: well, after all, your reasons can be understood. Four months pass. In Vienna, the Austrian Nazi Party stages a coup and invites German troops to enter Austria to guarantee security. On 12 March 1938 German troops enter Austria. Yes, the Anschluss, as it is called: the annexation.

Yes, the German army at that date is so unprepared for war that, according to Guderian’s retrospective account, roughly thirty percent of its tanks break down before they reach Vienna—not seventy percent of all military vehicles. [N-006] [C-007] Yet abroad nobody reacts.

In Austria there is popular enthusiasm; a plebiscite is held which approves annexation to Nazi Germany by ninety-nine percent. The cardinal of Vienna, Innitzer, declares that Austrian Catholics must thank the Lord and obey the new government, which has freed them from the communist threat: “We are also convinced that the work of the National Socialist movement has warded off the danger of utterly destructive, godless Bolshevism. The bishops accompany this work into the future with their best blessings and will also exhort the faithful in this spirit. On the day of the plebiscite it is for us bishops a self-evident national duty, as Germans, to declare ourselves for the German Reich, and we also expect all faithful Christians to know what they owe their people.” [Q-008] And abroad nobody bats an eyelid. [C-008]

In Britain, the London *Times* writes: after all, it is a little like what we did two centuries ago when Scotland joined England; these are fairly normal things. The people want it. Austria, it writes, “she was destined sooner or later to find herself in close association with the German Reich.” [Q-009]

Hitler takes note: Austria annexed, and nobody has reacted. A few days pass, but really only a few days. Still in that March of 1938—
