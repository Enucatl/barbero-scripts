## 3. Anschluss: engineered crisis and non-response

<!-- transcript: U-00182–U-00211; omissions: none -->

So Austria has a Nazi party that wants the country annexed to Germany and takes its orders from Hitler. At a certain point, Hitler gives the Austrian Nazis the go-ahead: stir up unrest, carry out attacks and acts of terrorism, create disorder, commit political murder. [C-006]

Then, finally, we reach March 1938. Four months have passed since Lord Halifax visited Berlin and told Hitler: well, after all, your position is understandable. Four months go by. In Vienna, the Austrian Nazi Party stages a coup and invites German troops into Austria to guarantee security. On 12 March 1938, German troops enter Austria. Yes, the Anschluss, as it's called: the annexation.

Yes, the German army is so unprepared for war at this point that, according to Guderian's later account, roughly thirty percent of its tanks break down before reaching Vienna—not seventy percent of all its military vehicles. [N-006] [C-007] And yet nobody abroad reacts.

In Austria, there's widespread enthusiasm. A plebiscite is held, and ninety-nine percent vote to approve annexation to Nazi Germany. The cardinal of Vienna, Innitzer, declares that Austrian Catholics must thank the Lord and obey the new government, which has saved them from the communist threat: “We are also convinced that the work of the National Socialist movement has warded off the danger of utterly destructive, godless Bolshevism. The bishops accompany this work into the future with their best blessings and will also exhort the faithful in this spirit. On the day of the plebiscite it is for us bishops a self-evident national duty, as Germans, to declare ourselves for the German Reich, and we also expect all faithful Christians to know what they owe their people.” [Q-008] And nobody abroad bats an eyelid. [C-008]

In Britain, the London *Times* writes: after all, it's rather like what we did two centuries ago, when Scotland joined England; this is all fairly normal. It's what the people wanted. Austria, it wrote, “she was destined sooner or later to find herself in close association with the German Reich.” [Q-009]

Hitler takes note: Austria annexed, and nobody reacts. A few days pass—really, only a few days. We're still in March 1938—
