## 4. Sudeten crisis and the exclusion of the Soviet Union

<!-- transcript: U-00212–U-00256; omissions: none -->

Hitler summons the leader of the Czechoslovak Nazis. Czechoslovakia also has a Nazi Party, drawn from the German minority in the Sudetenland. That’s what they’re called—I can never remember why—but the Germans in Czechoslovakia are known as the Sudeten Germans.

This Nazi Party among Czechoslovakia’s Germans takes its orders from Hitler. In March 1938, Hitler gives the signal: start the street violence, the agitation, the terrorism. Czechoslovakia begins to sink into chaos. [C-009]

The German press launches a relentless campaign. Germans in Czechoslovakia are being oppressed, it says. They’re a persecuted minority. The criminal Czechoslovak police arrest them, rape them, torture them. Germans in Czechoslovakia are suffering. This cannot go on. [Q-010]

Acting on instructions from their government, the British ambassadors in Berlin and Prague say: well, the Germans aren’t entirely wrong. Their position is understandable. They keep saying the German claims have some basis and that the Czechoslovak government needs to meet the Germans partway. We certainly don’t want to start another war, do we? So the Czechoslovak government had better make concessions. [C-010]

As the Nazis in Czechoslovakia start playing the same game they played in Austria, only one country takes a public stand. Only one calls on all the powers to come together and guarantee Czechoslovakia: the Soviet Union.

And of course no one listens, because the Soviet Union is the Bolshevik country, the communist country. It’s out of the game. During those weeks, the British ambassador in Moscow sends a report to London. He concludes, and I quote: “The Soviet Union must […] for the time being, be counted out of European politics in so far as the exercise of a decisive influence one way or the other is concerned.” [Q-011] They can treat Russia as if it doesn’t exist. [C-011]

Notice again how hard it is for them to grasp the way the world is changing. They keep talking about “European politics” as if Britain and France still make all the decisions. The United States isn’t there. Japan isn’t there. The Soviet Union isn’t there. Nothing. It’s still “European politics,” and Russia doesn’t count.

This goes on for months. Czechoslovakia is in chaos, and the German press keeps pounding away, pounding away, pounding away at the suffering of the Sudeten Germans. Meanwhile, Britain and France pressure the government in Prague: come on, make some concessions.
