## 15. Delayed Declarations and Fascist Propaganda

<!-- transcript: U-01096–U-01117; omissions: none -->

Another two days pass before Britain and France finally declare war on Germany. They still cling to the wild, naïve, but sincere hope that somehow this catastrophe can be avoided. The diplomacy continues, and both countries issue ultimatums. Britain's expires at eleven in the morning on September 3; France's expires at five that afternoon. [C-041]

Meanwhile, back in Italy, Mussolini is calm. Hitler has accepted that Italy is staying out, so the Italians wait to see whether they too can get what Ciano calls a share of the spoils. The Fascist government calls its position non-belligerency, not neutrality. And this is a conditional calculation, not a firm plan to annex territory. Ciano had laid it out in his diary on August 13 and 15: “13 August: 'The Duce's reactions are varied. At first he agrees with me. Then he says that honor obliges him to march with Germany. Finally he states that he wants his share of the spoils in Croatia and Dalmatia.' 15 August: 'For in his view it is still possible—though difficult—that the democracies will back down. In that case it would not suit us to have clashed with Germany, since we too must take our share of the spoils. We must therefore find a solution that allows us: (a) if the democracies attack, to disengage ourselves “honorably” from the Germans; (b) if the democracies swallow it, to seize the opportunity to settle accounts with Belgrade once and for all.'” [Q-046] [C-042]

Naturally, the Italian press gets its instructions. It can't say any of this. It has to tell a different story. So I'll close with the *Corriere della Sera* on September 1: “THE DECISIVE HOUR STRIKES / Hitler's proposals for Danzig and the Corridor—loyal, reasonable, and eminently feasible—foolishly allowed to lapse by Warsaw and London / England and company nailed to their tremendous responsibilities / The publication of this page of history confirms Germany's conscious wisdom and moderation in the face of the blind intransigence of the other parties to the conflict” [Q-047]. These are the newspapers our fathers were reading in this happy country of ours in 1939.

Thank you.
