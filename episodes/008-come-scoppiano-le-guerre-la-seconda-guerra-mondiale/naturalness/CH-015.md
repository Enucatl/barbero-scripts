## 15. Delayed Declarations and Fascist Propaganda

<!-- transcript: U-01096–U-01117; omissions: none -->

Another two days pass before Britain and France declare war on Germany. They are still clinging to the mad, naïve, but sincere hope that somehow this catastrophe can be avoided. In the meantime, there are ultimatums and further diplomatic exchanges. Britain's ultimatum expires at eleven in the morning on 3 September, and France's at five that afternoon. [C-041]

Meanwhile, in Italy, Mussolini is calm. Hitler has accepted that Italy will stay out, so they wait to see whether, as Ciano puts it, they too can claim a share of the spoils. The Fascist government calls its position non-belligerency, not neutrality, and the calculation is conditional rather than a settled plan for annexation. Ciano had recorded the idea on 13 and 15 August: “13 August: 'The Duce's reactions are varied. At first he agrees with me. Then he says that honor obliges him to march with Germany. Finally he states that he wants his share of the spoils in Croatia and Dalmatia.' 15 August: 'For in his view it is still possible—though difficult—that the democracies will back down. In that case it would not suit us to have clashed with Germany, since we too must take our share of the spoils. We must therefore find a solution that allows us: (a) if the democracies attack, to disengage ourselves “honorably” from the Germans; (b) if the democracies swallow it, to seize the opportunity to settle accounts with Belgrade once and for all.'” [Q-046] [C-042]

Naturally, the Italian press receives its instructions. It doesn't report any of this. It has to say something quite different. So I'll close with the *Corriere della Sera* of 1 September: “THE DECISIVE HOUR STRIKES / Hitler's proposals for Danzig and the Corridor—loyal, reasonable, and eminently feasible—foolishly allowed to lapse by Warsaw and London / England and company nailed to their tremendous responsibilities / The publication of this page of history confirms Germany's conscious wisdom and moderation in the face of the blind intransigence of the other parties to the conflict” [Q-047]. These are the newspapers our fathers were reading in this happy country of ours in 1939.

Thank you.
