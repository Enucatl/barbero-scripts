## 9. Ciano learns Germany wants war

<!-- transcript: U-00561–U-00595; omissions: none -->

In his diary entry for 4 August, Ciano writes: “I am beginning to consider the advisability of meeting Ribbentrop. The time has come to find out what the situation really is. The stakes are too high for us to wait passively for developments.” [Q-022]

So Ciano flies to Germany. On 11 August, he meets his German counterpart, Foreign Minister Joachim von Ribbentrop. Ciano has been sent to explain that starting a war now would be madness, and that they absolutely must not do it. In a later recollection, Ciano describes asking Ribbentrop as the two men walk together in the garden: “‘Well, Ribbentrop,’ I asked him as we walked side by side in the garden, ‘what do you want? The Corridor or Danzig?’ ‘Not anymore,’ and he fixed me with those cold Musée Grévin eyes of his, ‘we want war.’” [Q-023]

They’re at a reception, all standing around with champagne glasses in their hands. Ciano is desperate to tell his staff what Ribbentrop has just said to him, but the reception is still going on. Ciano later writes all this down. At last, he passes close to one of his aides and whispers in his ear, “We’re headed for a fight.”

Ciano talks further with Ribbentrop. The message is clear: war with Poland has been decided, and Poland will be crushed. And Britain? France? Ribbentrop believes they won’t intervene. And then comes the wager, just as in 1914—for those of you who were here last night. The wager is that they won’t intervene. Except this time—you know how history repeats itself, first as tragedy and then as farce?—they actually place a bet.

In Ciano’s later account: “They were making a fundamental error of calculation: they were certain that France and England would stand idly by while Poland was slaughtered. On this subject Ribbentrop wanted, during one of those gloomy meals we took at the Oesterreichischer Hof in Salzburg, to make a wager with me. I was to give him an Italian painting in the event of Anglo-French neutrality; he was to give me a collection of antique weapons in the event of intervention. Many witnesses were present, and not very long ago we were speaking about it with Ambassador von Mackensen. Ribbentrop, however, preferred to forget that wager and never paid his debt.” [Q-024] The idea is that Germany can go to war without setting off a world war, because France and Britain won’t dare intervene. [C-024]

The gala dinner ends, then the reception, the toasts, everything. Ciano returns to the hotel with his aides, desperate to tell them the whole story. Once they’re there, he starts wondering whether the Germans might have hidden microphones in the room. So they lock themselves in the bathroom, and there Ciano tells his aides that the Germans have now decided on war. And this is where the countdown begins: 12 August.
