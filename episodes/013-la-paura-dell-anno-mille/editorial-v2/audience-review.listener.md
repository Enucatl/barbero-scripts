## Priority listener-context issues

1. **CH-009: “the pope, sitting in the Vatican, still playing king and cutting off his opponents' heads.”** For an American listener who knows the pope principally as a religious leader, this introduces a large and startling piece of political history without orienting them. The subsequent reference to Italian unification assumes the same missing context. The point is intelligible as an attack, but the listener may pause to wonder whether the narrator means literal executions or metaphorical denunciation. A brief, source-compatible explanation that the pope also governed territory would carry substantial value here. Preserve the force of the passage; resolve the political context and whether the language is literal. This is the most useful place to add a few words rather than shorten.

2. **CH-010: “Because I see a militant defense of Christian culture in Gouguenheim's work” followed by “I'm not taking sides here.”** The final modern example names an ideological motive, but the narrated material has chiefly explained Gouguenheim's historical argument. Then “taking sides” has an unclear object: the historical argument about anxiety, or the political/religious camps? CH-009 has just made a clear political judgment, so the new disclaimer momentarily sounds inconsistent. Keep the modern example and make the boundary of this final judgment more explicit, if the source permits. This needs at most a small clarification, not an additional historical digression.

These are targeted clarity issues, not blockers to following the episode. Neither justifies a broad rewrite.
