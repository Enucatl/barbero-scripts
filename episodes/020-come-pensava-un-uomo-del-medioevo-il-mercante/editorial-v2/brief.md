### CH-005 · Il governo di popolo

### CH-006 · La guerra e gli ordinamenti di giustizia

U-00323–U-00416; original 21:45.345–28:41.360.

The violent destruction of Arezzo’s popular government and death in a cistern precede Florence’s Ordinances of Justice, collective family liability, armed enforcement and threatened priors. The definition of magnates through knightly kinship culminates in nobles’ fury: the men who won Campaldino have been excluded. Keep their angry quoted voice.

### CH-011 · Concordia, ragione e sconfitta

Dino longs for concord: the city could be rich and peaceful, yet brother attacks brother. Stage his Baptistery speech, common baptism, tears and oath, followed by immediate plotting from the most emotional men. Explain reason as God-given rather than an anti-religious modern discovery. Move to Cerchi versus Donati: commercial caution loses to practiced violent nobles. Retain Dino’s retrospective confession that mediation failed and they should have sharpened swords. Keep Barbero’s skepticism about Dino’s self-portrait and brief time-check.

### CH-012 · Religione, imperatore e giustizia di Dio

U-00713–U-00801; original 49:40.680–55:45.755.

Ask where religion has been in this brutal politics. Faith matters inwardly, but oaths and the procession fail as political remedies; Dino later fears having led perjurers into damnation. The sharpen-the-swords refrain returns as mockery. Defeat sends minor merchants back to business; great financiers ally with nobles, Dante is exiled, Dino stays. Henry VII briefly offers order from outside, exposing the surrender of civic self-government. Finally the long-lived chronicler watches enemies die and takes consolation in divine justice, including the graphic gangrene anecdote. Preserve the closing refusal of easy parallels with today and the thanks; l’oggi is an approved recognition repair.
