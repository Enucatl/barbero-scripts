## 2. America in 1860: Growth and the North-South Divide

<!-- chapter: CH-002; transcript: U-00086–U-00198 -->

In 1860 the United States has 31,000,000 inhabitants. Today we might laugh at that, today they have 300,000,000, but for the middle of the nineteenth century it is a very substantial population. 31,000,000 inhabitants means that the United States has more inhabitants than Great Britain, though France’s population is nearly six million larger, and I am talking about the two greatest world powers, the two countries at the forefront in Europe that already have vast colonial empires in the rest of the world, so the United States is potentially already one of the great world powers. [N-003] But the most impressive thing is this: in 1860 they have 31,000,000 inhabitants, but ten years earlier they had only 23,000,000. Which means that in one decade the population has grown by a third. The country is growing enormously, filling up with immigrants, and experiencing colossal industrial growth. The central decades of the nineteenth century, as you know, are decades of overwhelming industrial growth in the countries at the forefront—in Great Britain, Germany, France, the United States. In that decade, 1850–60, the value of manufacturing output in the United States rises by about 85 percent. When one talks about figures, it is good to stop for a moment and reflect: you know how we struggle with 0.5 percent a year? One percent annual growth? Well, here we are talking about an increase of about 85 percent over the course of ten years. For the moment, it is still a country in which farmers outnumber city dwellers, but the overtaking is already about to come. In 1860 there are about 49,000 kilometres of functioning railways in the United States, plus those under construction: about 49,000 kilometres. In all of Italy, on the same date, 1,800 kilometres. [N-004] What is more, what is more, it is a society unlike any other in the world, because by 1860 most states have broadly enfranchised white men, although voting rules remain state-based and exclude women, most Black people, and others. Yet there is a women’s movement demanding equal rights and the vote for women too. White literacy is about 90 percent among free white adults under the census measure. Wages are comparatively high, but wealth inequality remains substantial; whereas today the United States, as we know, is among Western countries the one where inequality between the super-rich and the poor is greatest, even though we in Italy are rapidly catching up with them. The literacy rate is extremely high. In Italy, on the same date, 20 percent. United States, 90. Everyone reads the newspaper. [N-005]

Now, this overall picture is true of the whole country, but only up to a point: the talk about democracy, egalitarianism, literacy applies throughout the country, even though perhaps in the North these factors are even more pronounced than in the South. But the enormous industrial development, immigration, the mass of people arriving, development and constant transformation characterize only the North. The North has three quarters of the inhabitants, but almost all the factories, almost all the workers. The South is rich, but it is an entirely agricultural country, where wealth consists of plantations and slaves, you understand? 4,000,000 slaves are an immense capital asset. The South owns 4,000,000 slaves, so on paper it possesses colossal wealth, and it is fabulously rich thanks to the profits from cotton, which has by then become a monoculture in the South. There is still some tobacco in Virginia, but whereas the South a century earlier had been a country where tobacco, sugar, rice were produced, in 1860 the South is the country of cotton, and it is the country of cotton because the Industrial Revolution is under way in England. The mills of Manchester consume a mountain of cotton every year, and this cotton comes from the Southern states. Thanks to the capital tied up in 4,000,000 slaves and the enormous profits from cotton, the South is fabulously rich, but it is a stagnant economy that is not growing, is not transforming itself. The North, on the other hand, is undergoing an extremely profound transformation.

And yet until the middle of the century, the South leads politically, directs politically. The elite of the great landowners, the Southern planters, has dominated the politics of the United States. Lincoln is the sixteenth president. Of the fifteen presidents before Lincoln, nine were born in the Southern states, and all nine had at some point held people in slavery. You understand? Until Lincoln’s election, a little more than half of the presidents of the United States had personally held people in slavery. And even those who came from the North had always been elected on the basis of electoral agreements with the Southern planters. There had never been a president who opposed the interests of the South. [N-006]
