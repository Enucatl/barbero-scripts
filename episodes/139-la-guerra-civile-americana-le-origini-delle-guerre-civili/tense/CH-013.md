## 13. Lincoln’s Election and Southern Secession

<!-- chapter: CH-013; transcript: U-01244–U-01312 -->

The result is that Lincoln wins the election in November, but he wins it by winning the North and the West, and in nine Southern states, as I have been telling you, he does not get even a single vote. Lincoln receives no popular votes in nine Southern states where presidential voting occurs because Republican tickets are often unavailable and the party has little or no organization there; public voting and local pressure can also discourage open support. [N-030] But he has been elected. He will be the next president; the Republicans have won in November. On 20 December, South Carolina proclaims its independence. And before Lincoln’s inauguration in March, six other Southern states leave the Union, and then they all join together and form the Confederacy. We are at the end now, but let me quote you a few more passages from President Lincoln’s inaugural address, 4 March 1861. “I have no purpose, directly or indirectly, to interfere with the institution of slavery in the States where it exists. I believe I have no lawful right to do so, and I have no inclination to do so. [...] I hold, that in contemplation of universal law, and of the Constitution, the Union of these States is perpetual. [...] no State, upon its own mere motion, can lawfully get out of the Union. [...] I therefore consider that, in view of the Constitution and the laws, the Union is unbroken; and, to the extent of my ability, I shall take care [...] that the laws of the Union be faithfully executed in all the States. [...] Plainly, the central idea of secession, is the essence of anarchy. A majority, held in restraint by constitutional checks, and limitations [...] is the only true sovereign of a free people. Whoever rejects it, does, of necessity, fly to anarchy or to despotism.” Physically speaking, this is still Lincoln’s inaugural address, “Physically speaking, we cannot separate. We cannot remove our respective sections from each other, nor build an impassable wall between them.” They are not yet Trump’s times, evidently. “A husband and wife may be divorced, and go out of the presence, and beyond the reach of each other; but the different parts of our country cannot do this.” And Lincoln, in fact, throughout the whole speech, Lincoln is addressing himself exclusively to those in the South. He is addressing himself exclusively to those in the South to persuade them to turn back. “In your hands, my dissatisfied fellow countrymen, and not in mine, is the momentous issue of civil war. The government will not assail you. You can have no conflict, without being yourselves the aggressors. You have no oath registered in Heaven to destroy the government, while I shall have the most solemn one to preserve, protect and defend it.” And then he ends like this. “I am loth to close. We are not enemies, but friends. We must not be enemies.” As a result of Lincoln’s inauguration, the Southern states declare that all federal property on their territory is confiscated,
