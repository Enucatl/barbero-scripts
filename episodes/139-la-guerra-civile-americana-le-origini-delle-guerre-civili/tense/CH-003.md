## 3. Three Regions and Westward Expansion

<!-- chapter: CH-003; transcript: U-00199–U-00276 -->

And this political hegemony of the South, in reality, how shall I put it, is explained by the fact that although so far I have spoken of North and South, in reality the United States is not divided into two; it is divided into three, because there is also the West, or as they call it, the West. The North, the North with Puritan New England at its heart, Connecticut, Massachusetts, and with the great metropolis of New York. The North is commercial, port-based, business-minded, industrial, in tumultuous demographic growth, dominated by commercial and industrial capitalism. The South is agricultural, it produces cotton, then sends it to the North because it does not even have good ports. The South in fact lives off that, it is in the hands of an elite of planters and lives on an economy based on slavery. And then there is the West, because the American continent is still in large part to be colonized. In the eyes of whites, it belongs to nobody. We know that innumerable Indian nations live there, to use the term once used, indigenous, native nations. In the eyes of whites, it is as though nobody were there. It is a world to conquer. The West they are conquering in those years is not yet the Far West of cowboys; it is what today is called the Middle West, a country of settlers, of farmers who arrive there and there is land for everyone. Settlers acquire or claim western land under several federal and state systems, commonly involving purchase or pre-emption; the federal Homestead Act providing free homesteads will come only in 1862. [N-007] Obviously, this too is an egalitarian society, the most egalitarian in America; it is an individualistic society and it is a peasant society. The fact that the West is a world of farmers explains why for a long time the West and the South are allied, because fundamentally they have the same interests: they are interested in defending agriculture and not industrial interests, and then they are interested in territorial expansion. Territorial expansion means that America and the United States must find room for the flood of immigrants who are arriving; they must expand westward, and the Southern planters too have a great desire to expand westward. The West, the West is large, but then there is the whole Southwest. The war against Mexico in 1848, of which we remember nothing. Who knows that the United States goes to war against Mexico in 1848? At most we remember Fort Alamo, Davy Crockett from some Hollywood film. The war of 1848 is an epoch-making moment in which the United States, by defeating Mexico, confirms the Texas boundary and takes possession of immense territories: New Mexico, California. Texas had already been annexed and admitted to the United States in 1845. [N-008] And that war is wanted by the Southern planters and the Western settlers, allied and represented in politics by a party that still exists today, the Democratic Party. It may seem strange to us that the party of the Southern Planters is the Democratic Party. In reality, those who are my age remember that even in the 1960s the South votes Democratic. Then with Reagan it begins voting Republican and now it is the stronghold of the Republicans and of Trump, but for a long time in the South they take it for granted that their party is the Democratic Party. In the nineteenth century the party of the South is the Democratic Party, and it is also the party of the settlers, the small landowners of the West. Except that this coalition, in the middle of the nineteenth century, begins to be threatened, because in the face of the coalition of farmers and planters, the industrialists of the North count for more and more, and they want policies different from those that suit agriculture.
