In a private 1773 letter, Patrick Henry writes: “What adds to the wonder is, that this abominable practice has been introduced in the most enlightened ages.” In a Senate speech in 1826, John Randolph of Roanoke says: “It is a cancer on your face, and must not be tampered with by quacks, who never saw the disease or the patient, and prescribe across the Atlantic.” [N-016] And as late as 1832, a fateful year which I realize I have already mentioned several times, the Virginia assembly, the Virginia Parliament, the most important state in the South, discusses a motion for the abolition of slavery. It does not pass, but by only a few votes. It does not pass, above all because while at first everyone may well agree that sooner or later slavery will have to be overcome, in practical terms, in practical terms, what do we do then? The slaves, we have said, are an enormous capital asset. It is not as if you can, how shall I put it, private property is sacred, guys, we are in America. And it is not as if you can take an enormous piece of private property away from people without compensating the owners? And with what money? And above all, once you have freed the Blacks, what do you tell them to do? The debate founders there, because in reality nobody imagines that Blacks can go on living as free citizens in the United States alongside whites. They have to be sent somewhere. In the first decades of the nineteenth century, when abolition of slavery is discussed in the South, societies are also founded to send free Blacks back to Africa. That is the solution: humanitarian societies that raise capital and do it. The American Colonization Society establishes Liberia in 1822 through agreements and coercive expansion, and begins financing the transfer to Africa of freed slaves. All this takes place under the presidency of Monroe, who is one of the Southern presidents and slave owners, who strongly encourages this project to send Blacks back to Africa, so much so that the capital of Liberia, which after a few years becomes an independent state, will be called, and is still called, as you know, Monrovia, in honor of President Monroe, a slave owner and advocate of sending slaves back to Africa as we manage to free them. In a few decades they send back to Liberia something like 15,000 freed Blacks, who moreover later dominate Indigenous populations through unequal political authority, coerced labor, and systems of dependency, but that is another matter.
