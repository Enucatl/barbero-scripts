## Quotation and attributed-anecdote targets

Each numbered item is a discovery grouping. The coordinator should split speakers and document boundaries into separate quote records, particularly the father scene and royal visit. “Confirmed” below means the attribution/content is found in Salimbene, not that a supernatural event or hostile anecdote happened as told.

### Reading, memory, paper, prophecy

1. **U101–113: remembered satirical song; “I cannot remember it all.”** Cantarelli I52; Bernini Pars II, section on Boncompagno and Giovanni da Vicenza. Original recovered: `quendam rithmum fecit in derisionem fratris Iohannis de Vincentia, cuius nec principii reminiscor nec finis, quia multa tempora sunt, quod non legi ipsum, et quando legi, non bene commendavi memorie, quia nec multum curabam. Erant autem ibi verba ista, prout memorie occurrunt:`. Direct sense: he composed a rhyme mocking Giovanni; Salimbene remembers neither beginning nor end, having not read it for a long time, and did not commit it well to memory because he did not care much. This confirms selective memory, not the lecture's universal claim that medieval readers memorized everything. The unrecited song begins `Et Iohannes iohanniçat / et saltando choreizat`; do not insert song into adaptation simply because recovered.

2. **U127–131: too poor for writing material.** Cantarelli I122: `Dopo deposi la penna, e la troncai lì, perchè io era tanto povero che mi mancava sin la carta o la pergamena. Ed ora volge l'anno 1284.` English from this Italian translation: “Then I laid down my pen and broke off there, because I was so poor that I lacked even paper or parchment. And now the year is1284.” Refers to chronicle composed at Ferrara in1250 reaching the Lombard domination; not necessarily loss of all writing for the rest of his life. Original Latin deferred. U126 paper “just invented” needs independent chronology before correction; invention and Italian diffusion are different claims.

3. **U145–195: Joachim,1260, abandonment, Frederick, pope.** Cantarelli I77 and discussions around I181 and I326–327 support Salimbene's Joachimist interests, eventual repudiation of the timetable, and Frederick-death crisis. Exact Latin and quotation boundaries deferred. Birth1221 makes him29 at Frederick's death in1250 and30 at the1251 Ferrara announcement. Do not reconcile lecture1220/30 by altering transcript. Canon2, LateranIV contradicts unrestricted “Church never ruled” if read literally, but concerns Trinitarian doctrine, not a blanket condemnation of all his prophecy. “Age of Spirit” scheme and future Franciscan mission should be attributed as the lecture's simplification of Joachimist currents; authentic Joachim and later followers/pseudonymous texts need distinction. The pope proximity claim is about being able to touch him, not clear evidence he actually did.

4. **U202–219: unnamed weaver's prophecies and circulation.** Cantarelli I335–336. Context is Salimbene's discussion with Gerardo da Borgo San Donnino. Weaver is **from Verona, living in Parma**, already known to Salimbene and already read by him. `Li dà di buon grado, e va in sollucchero quando glieli cercano` supports eagerness to distribute manuscripts. “Publishing” is a modern analogy. The lecture's first-discovery/first-visit framing is a departure from this translation. Recover Latin before authoritative rewriting.

5. **U220–253: Alberto's erasure, reasons and Salimbene's reaction.** Cantarelli I335–337. **Alberto Cremonella** entered Franciscan novitiate with Salimbene in1238, **left**, learned medicine, later became a Cistercian at Fontevivo. Thus “Franciscan lent to Cistercians” is not this account. Separate quoted targets: offer of Bernard's books; request for prophecy; announcement of death; erasure; learning/practising scraping; protection from scandal; Salimbene's regret. Translation gives `de' suoi scritti neppure una sillaba rimase al mondo; perchè io di mia mano ho abraso tutti gli scritti suoi`. He says no syllable remains, but this is a participant's sweeping claim, not an independently demonstrated census of manuscripts. Immediately afterward Salimbene advises burning **Gerardo's** dangerous book. That complicates a claim that he simply opposed suppression. His comparison with Jeremiah's destroyed scroll is textual moral judgment; neither participant should be reduced to modern free-speech positions. Original Latin deferred.

6. **U274–292: possessed man, demon and bad Latin.** Exact primary passage **not recovered**. Search-indexed2020 intercomprehension scholarship points to the same lector/demon incident. **Do not substitute Cantarelli II134**, a different story involving novice Pietro and a demon speaking excellent Latin. Separate demon's grammatical defense and friar's learned rebuke remain deferred; faithful contextual paraphrase can preserve Barbero's attribution, but no recovered-wording replacement is yet eligible. Supernatural causation remains part of narrated belief.

7. **U297: “I know the Bible; you cannot fool me.”** Potential composite, not recovered as a single identical sentence. Cantarelli I334: `Dimmi dunque in quale punto, perchè la Bibbia la conosco bene.` Immediately afterward he fetches the Bible and opens it toIsaiah18. Confirms his self-confidence, but also qualifies sweeping U101 suggestion that disputation never required consulting books. Do not fabricate a Latin epigram from Barbero's wording.

### Friars, rivals, family

8. **U307–315: Giovanni da Vicenza miracles and beard relics.** Cantarelli I53–54; Bernini Pars II. Found accusation that Giovanni thought he could work true miracles without God's help; indignation when cut beard hairs were not saved. This is Salimbene's partisan characterization of a Dominican, not independent access to Giovanni's intentions. Separate miracle claim and beard complaint. Original full spans can be taken from ParsII after coordinator chooses excerpt; no need impose them on lecture.

9. **U318–347: Diotisalvi and tunic in latrine.** Cantarelli I53–55; Coulton AppendixC prints Latin cited to MGH79. Name **frater Deustesalvet** in Latin; Italian **frate Diotisalvi da Fiorenza**. Key original boundaries: `nisi ei darent de tunica fratris Johannis ... ut pro reliquiis reservaret`; `post prandium purgato ventre posteriora terxit, et petiam dejecit in stercora`; `Heu, heu! succurrite, fratres, quia reliquias sancti requiro, quas perdidi in latrina!`; `vultus suos inclinassent ad orificia camerarum`; `stercora revolvebat valenter`; `erubuerunt cognoscentes se a tali trufatore delusos`. Direct sense: asks for tunic cloth as relic; after dinner wipes his backside and throws cloth into excrement; calls for help finding saint's relics lost in privy; friars peer through openings; he stirs excrement energetically; they recognize the trick and blush. These are separated excerpt fragments, **not one continuous quotation**. Latin supports crude joke. Cantarelli's rendering of viewing openings is imperfect; do not retrofit exact staging from translation. Florentines as great tricksters is Salimbene's ethnic/civic stereotype, not fact about all Florentines.

10. **U361–400: Franciscan/Dominican learning versus Apostolics.** Cantarelli I153–167, especially156, confirms fierce rivalry for alms and repeated allegations of ignorance and lack of useful services: Apostolics neither pray for donors nor celebrate mass, preach, confess, or give good advice/examples. These are narrator's hostile claims. Salimbene calls locals generous to rivals; not evidence for a measured revenue loss. Separate descriptions from imagined composite dialogue. **U402–407 penitentiagite** exact spelling/locator was not securely recovered in this checkpoint; Latin critical page still needed. **U408–411 Eco's reuse** not independently checked against *The Name of the Rose*; leave as Barbero's literary observation rather than verified borrowing of the whole passage.

11. **U429–431: Parmesan thirty-year vengeance.** Cantarelli II86: `Nota che i Parmigiani, uno de' quali mi son io, dicono che la vendetta sino a trent'anni è ancora in tempo. E dicono vero.` Direct English from Italian: “Note that the people of Parma, of whom I am one, say that revenge is still timely after thirty years. And they speak truly.” Confirmed in substance; original Latin deferred. It is proverb/authorial assent, not quantified judicial practice.

12. **U432–451: father and endangered lineage.** Cantarelli I15–20,35–36. Two living legitimate sons become friars; source also records son Niccolò dying as a child and illegitimate Giovanni, plus daughters. “Two sons” is intelligible in the legitimate-heir argument but not a complete child count. Scholarship calls the father a notary; family also had local elite/knightly connections. “Highest aristocracy” should not be silently treated as a verified technical rank. Further prosopographical checking needed before intervention.

13. **U452–469: imperial letter and return condition.** Cantarelli I17–18; Federiciana entry cites imperial request incipit `Ad Guidonis de Adam fidelis nostri suspiria mitiganda`. Salimbene says he later saw Frederick's letter copied by Illuminato. **The conditional instruction that he return if he wishes belongs to Elias's letter**, not necessarily Frederick's. Distinguish two documentary objects, later recollection of imperial request, and friars' oral response. No original imperial charter was independently inspected. Quoted “if he wants to” must not be assigned to emperor as recovered primary wording.

14. **U470–494: confrontation dialogue, scripture, insult, curse.** Cantarelli I18–20; Bernini I54 scan/transcription; Coulton AppendixC, MGH40. Separate voices: custodian Geremia; father; Salimbene/scriptural citations; friars; father curse. Recovered insult: `Fili dilecte, non credas istis pissintunicis` — explanatory narrator clause `id est qui in tunicis mingunt` — `qui te deceperunt`. Direct English: “Dear son, do not believe these piss-in-tunics”—that is, those who urinate in their tunics—“who have deceived you.” **Non credas**, not *non credere*; **pissintunicis** printed as one word; **id est**. This establishes source spelling, not acoustics. The explanation is narratorial, not necessarily spoken by father. Curse begins `Comendo te mille demonibus, maledicte fili, et fratrem tuum, qui hic tecum est`: “I commend you to a thousand demons, accursed son, and your brother who is here with you.” Biblical reply is a **composite**: Luke9:62;Matthew10:37–38 and10:34–36;Psalm26/27:10;Jeremiah3:19;Lamentations3:27 in this scene. Lecture's “leave father and mother and follow me” is not safely one exact Gospel quotation. Preserve signalled paraphrase unless separately recovered and approved.

15. **U498–510: dream of Virgin and infant.** Cantarelli I20–21. Mary on altar offers child to kiss; child spreads arms; Salimbene embraces and kisses him. `nel mio cuore ne rimase una ineffabile dolcezza; e veramente confesso che non ebbi mai nel secolo a provare tanta delizia.` Translation: “An indescribable sweetness remained in my heart; and I truly confess that I had never experienced such delight in the world.” Recollection of a dream, not externally verified apparition. Separate the child's actions from retrospective comparison.

16. **U513–524: hypothetical bishopric to comfort father.** Cantarelli I35–36; Bernini ParsII: `Quod papa non fecisset, ut puto. Forte ad solatium patris mei michi episcopatum dedisset vel aliquam dignitatem.` Direct sense: “The pope would not have done that, I think. Perhaps to comfort my father he would have given me a bishopric or some dignity.” A hypothetical retrospective speculation, **not an actual papal offer**. Pope's family/Parmesan acquaintance is relevant context.

17. **U530–557: Pisa, insult, begging dream.** Cantarelli I26–28. Actual encounter with a Parmesan follows a garden musical performance. Rebuke invokes abundance of bread/meat among servants and knightly tournaments, and accuses friars of being spectacles for women and diversion for performers. Exact “money to minstrels” not established by this wording. **Street/Fondaco avoidance is in the subsequent dream**, not necessarily literal routine reported for that encounter. In dream Jesus, Mary, **and Joseph put bread into his bag**: `Gesù Cristo usciva di una casa, e mi portava pane, e me lo poneva nella sporta. Altrettanto faceva la beata Vergine, altrettanto Giuseppe`. Lecture U553–557 says Jesus/Mary themselves begging with bags; **substantive action differs**. Further Latin check advisable; do not fix Italian transcript as an acoustic error.

18. **U564–571: cabbage for the rest of his life.** Cantarelli II229. First-day lavish food contrasted with later cabbage: `Ma in seguito mi alimentarono di cavoli per tutta vita mia, quantunque da secolare io non avessi mai mangiato cavoli, anzi mi movevano tanta ripugnanza, che non avrei nemmeno mangiate carni che fossero state cotte con cavoli`. Confirmed comic retrospective exaggeration. The same chronicle describes numerous later banquets; do not interpret “all my life” as literal exclusive diet.

### Bad clergy, wine, hospitality

19. **U589–593: unbelieving bishop.** Cantarelli I42; Bernini ParsII. **Gregorius Romanus**, died Mantua. Original: `mortuus est Mantue hereticus et maledictus`; when offered sacrament `noluit illud sumere, dicens quod nichil credebat de tali fide`; asked why he accepted bishopric, `dixit quod propter divitias et honores; et sic obiit et non communicavit`. Direct sense: refused to receive it, saying he believed nothing of that faith; accepted for wealth and honors; died without communion. “Atheist” is a modern interpretive label stronger than disbelief in this faith. This is Salimbene's hostile report, not a witnessed/verbatim deathbed transcript.

20. **U595–598: ipse viderit.** Exact repeated Latin occurrences/count not recovered. Cantarelli I26 has analogous “egli se lo saprà” about a vanished lay friar. Do not assert numerous occurrences on this single match. The phrase's nonjudgmental force is interpretation; its literal sense is roughly “let him see to it / that is his concern.” Deferred original target.

21. **U599–609: Filippo of Ravenna, children, prebends and wine.** Cantarelli I294 supports nephews/son and sale of prebends: a person filling his hands could get one; source also describes a daughter and marriage complication. **Convent-daughter and wine-bottles vignette not recovered** in this translation. Treat the nepotism and the wine vignette as separate targets, latter deferred. Do not use a broadly corrupt portrait as proof of every detail. Salimbene also records courtesy/generosity and violent anger, requiring care with “never judges.”

22. **U613–619: Elias remains seated at fire, cushions and hat.** Exact primary incident not located at checkpoint. Broader criticism of Elias/luxury occurs in chronicle, but cannot authenticate this particular staging. Deferred, eligible only for contextual lecture rendering pending Latin source.

23. **U632–635: English king distributes an excellent wine.** Cantarelli II242. Account located in discussion of prelates/hospitality. **King unnamed in inspected passage**; do not add HenryIII or other identification. Fountain/distribution anecdote is exemplary reported conduct, not necessarily witnessed by Salimbene. Original Latin and exact continuous excerpt deferred.

24. **U639–643: bishops keep best wine; throats are sisters.** Cantarelli II242. Same hospitality discussion supports rebuke and kinship analogy. Distinguish narrator's general maxim from particular king anecdote. Original Latin deferred; proposed authoritative English should depend on retrieved Latin, not a newly Latinized slogan.

25. **U644–658: Burgundian wines and tastes.** Cantarelli I122–125; observations include Auxerre, quantity/strength/quality and comparison with Italian wines. First-person tasting and local preference support anecdote. Lecture's classwide equation white=noble/red=peasant is broader than demonstrated by the passages inspected; wine color and class should remain Salimbene/Barbero's observation, not universal medieval rule.

26. **U659–665: French drinking and water in eyes.** Cantarelli I125. Named speaker **frate Bartolomeo Guiscolo da Parma**, at **Provins**, heard repeatedly by Salimbene. Printed vernacular: `Alè ke maletta ve don Dè; metti del aighe in les vin, non in les ocli`. Translator then glosses: `Andate che Dio vi mandi alla malora; mettete acqua nel vino, non negli occhi.` Direct sense: “Go, may God send you to the devil; put water in your wine, not in your eyes.” Mixed/represented French spelling belongs to this edition, not standardized modern French. Separate narrator's drinking stereotype, devotional eye-washing practice, and Bartolomeo's joke.

27. **U665–669: eight French custodies, four wine/four beer.** Cantarelli I123 confirms eightfold geographical contrast, but calls units “conventi.” This may be the old translation's loose terminology; **do not correct the lecture's custodie to conventi on this basis**. Latin institutional term and contemporary province boundaries deferred.

### Louis, banquets, prejudice and observations

28. **U673–686: Louis's crusade itinerary and arrival at Sens.** Cantarelli I126–130: Pentecost1248 visit to Franciscans at **Sens**, arrival on foot attested. No support here for months walking the entire kingdom. **Aigues-Mortes** is a plausible contextual place-name, but its primary locator was not directly recovered in this checkpoint. Do not use historical plausibility to settle acoustic spelling.

29. **U687–691: enormous pike gift.** Cantarelli I128–129, royal-visit sequence. Gift presented by treasurer of church of Sens; not enough to establish bishop personally sent it. Carried in a vessel compared with infant bath. French esteem for pike is attested; Salimbene's personal dislike is not explicitly established. Barbero's comic reading is interpretation. Original Latin for the speech deferred.

30. **U692–700: royal prayer, poverty speech and tears.** Cantarelli I129–130. **Louis** recommends himself, brothers and queen mother to friars' prayers. **John of Parma's reply** contains the **no gold or silver** formula. Lecture appears to put that formula in Louis's mouth: speaker assignment requires editorial decision, not automatic replacement. Some French people present weep; “all French” is comic generalization. Separate three quotation boundaries: king's request; minister's reply; narrator's tears observation. Acts3:6 allusion does not authorize borrowing a modern Bible translation into Louis's speech.

31. **U704–723: king's banquet menu.** Cantarelli I130. Primary translation lists cherries; very white bread; wine; fresh beans cooked in milk; fish and crabs; eel pastries; rice with almond milk and cinnamon; roasted eels with excellent sauce; tarts; curds/cheeses; abundant fruit. Confirms unusual detailed list. Lecture's eel “polpette” versus translation's “pasticci” needs Latin culinary noun before correction. Preserve food-list boundaries, not every surrounding joke as Salimbene quotation.

32. **U730–739: forty courses, patriarch of Aquileia.** Cantarelli I166: `il primo dì di quaresima, fa servire alla sua mensa quaranta pietanze ... sino al sabbato santo ... diminuire ... di una ogni giorno`; motive honor/glory of patriarchate. Source says Holy Saturday, not Good Friday. This is Salimbene's report and moral criticism, not audited menu records; he calls it superfluous. Do not silently repair arithmetic/calendar or narrator's reported custom.

33. **U745–751: Norman speech and insult toward southerners.** Cantarelli I245–246. Context includes a reported speech ascribed to Robert, followed by Salimbene's own characterization. Nardone, citing Scalia1966 p522, gives `homines caccareli et merdaçoli`. Barbero's2011 printed preview gives `homines caccarelli et merdaçòli`. Edition spellings differ; do not collapse them into a fabricated single critical reading. U751 scatological original target should retain exact edition provenance. Medieval narrative here also has tangled Norman genealogy; the comic military comparison does not establish factual southern cowardice or a simple cause of conquest.

34. **U755: “what do you want?” southern speech.** Cantarelli I245 prints **`Ke bulì?`**; Barbero's2011 preview **`Ke boli?`**. Search-indexed scholarly Latin has `quando volunt dicere: “Quid vis?”, dicunt: “Ke boli?”`, but full source not opened, so **not recovered authoritative original Latin**. Good source-recognition context for “caboli”, not acoustic proof. Lecture imitates sound; spelling intervention must distinguish recognized dialect from criticism of historical accuracy.

35. **U759–769: French tu, Lombard voi.** Cantarelli II243 says French `Tu Messere` even to pope/emperor; Lombards use voi to a child, hen, **merlo (blackbird)** and stick. Lecture has **cat**: lexical departure from this translation, pending Latin confirmation. Nonexistence/later emergence of polite Lei needs separate language-history source; not checked. Neither passage is a systematic survey of all speakers. Preserve quoted forms without turning observer's exaggeration into universal linguistic law.

36. **U778–787: ravioli without pasta.** Cantarelli II106, under1284: `mangiai per la prima volta, nel giorno di S. Chiara, i ravioli senza involucro di pasta`. Direct sense: “For the first time, on St Clare's day, I ate ravioli without a pasta covering.” VoSLIG attests Latin lemma **`raviolus sine crusta de pasta`**, through Sella1937 p288; this lexical citation is not the complete chronicle sentence. Naked dumplings/filling are historically plausible; Barbero explicitly voices uncertainty and conjectures another preparation. Do not silently convert his conjecture into a firm recipe. ## Central interpretations and selected checks

- **Salimbene gives access to a medieval mind:** useful interpretive thesis, not representative-sample proof. He is one unusually articulate, literate male friar, composing retrospectively and drawing on documents and literary sources. Anecdote-rich memoir should not be treated as a contemporaneous stenographic diary. Paul's article and DBI supply scholarly context, with access limits noted above.
- **Faith, learning and humour coexist:** richly supported by juxtaposed passages. No need “correct” this literary argument. Claims of universal memory, universal credulity, uniformly illiterate rivals or unique modern scientific curiosity are broader rhetorical extrapolations.
- **His religious vocation is sincere despite luxury and crude humour:** a plausible reading of reported dreams, persistence and self-description, not a fact independently measurable from external documentation. Do not recast jokes as proof he lacked faith.
- **He rarely judges and respects others' choices:** contested characterization. “Ipse viderit,” charitable impressions and amusement coexist with explicit denunciation, prejudice and proposals to burn texts. This is an interpretive tension, not a simple factual error. Record counterevidence without replacing the lecture's organizing argument.
- **Joachim chronology:** birth9 October1221; profession4 February1238; Frederick dies1250; news episode1251; crucial disappointed timetable1260; final writing largely1280s. Source narration may move freely among dates. A change to spoken dates or ages needs a source/editorial decision.
- **Franciscan identity:** mendicant order, preaching, study and rivalry well supported. Elite family, institutional poverty and actual lavish meals can coexist. Avoid literalizing cabbage-for-life or turning an old translator's “conventi” into institutional correction.
- **LouisIX:** Sens1248 and walking into the city established. Sainthood, later crusade death and exact departure route should be separately sourced if materially asserted; this checkpoint does not pretend a complete royal biography audit.
- **Frederick's alleged experiment:** strongest central evidential limitation. Attribution securely established; event's occurrence unresolved. Distinguish humanistic literary contrast from a verified causal experiment showing emotional deprivation caused all deaths.
