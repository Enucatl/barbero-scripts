## 9. Modern flat-earthers and the duty to verify

<!-- chapter: CH-009; transcript: U-00985–U-01035 -->

<!-- research: [Q-015] [Q-016] [C-015] [C-016] -->

So everyone knew the Earth was round. Then who writes the following demonstration? “Human beings require a surface on which to live that, in its general character, shall be LEVEL; and since the Omniscient Creator must have been perfectly acquainted with the requirements of His creatures, it follows that, being an All-wise Creator, He has met them thoroughly. This is a theological proof that the Earth is not a globe.”

Had I opened by attributing that passage to a doctor of Salamanca, I suspect everyone would have accepted it. It comes instead from a nineteenth-century American, William Carpenter, who in 1885 self-publishes in Baltimore—American publishing deserves credit for that restraint—a book called *One Hundred Proofs That the Earth Is Not a Globe*. Carpenter's 1885 pamphlet belongs to a nineteenth-century flat-Earth revival that had already developed around Samuel Rowbotham in England. [N-006] The hundred proofs are extraordinary and available online. One says that if you rise in a balloon and look down, the Earth plainly appears flat.

A Flat Earth Society, Barbero says, still exists in America. Its thousands of adherents believe Earth is flat and that authorities deceive us about it, just as they supposedly fabricated the Moon landing. Perhaps for the first time, people believe in a flat Earth because they believe everything they are told forms part of a conspiracy.

Thinking about this, I worry that I may seem to have done the same thing during these evenings. I hope I have not told you that anything you hear is likely false. What I hope I have said is that we should not believe a claim merely because someone tells us or because we read it. If it matters to us and we truly want to believe it, then within the limits of our abilities we must try to check it. Thank you.
