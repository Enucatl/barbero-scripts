# Episode outline

The lecture explains the 1982 Falklands War as the product of two political gambles made by
Argentina's military junta: that recovering the islands would restore its domestic legitimacy and
that Britain would accept the fait accompli. Barbero then follows the documentary trail through
British surprise, American mediation, Thatcher's decision to dispatch the fleet, and the radically
different political consequences of Argentina's defeat and Britain's victory.

## 1. The islands, their names, and 150 years of dispute

- Transcript: U-00001–U-00119; original audio 00:51–07:25.
- Argument: the islands' remoteness and rival names embody opposed sovereignty claims; the coming
  150th anniversary of British possession made the issue politically urgent in Argentina.
- Narrative: declassification motivates the lecture; geography, population, British possession
  from 1833, and Argentine political education establish the dispute.
- Aside: Barbero acknowledges that choosing “Falkland” over “Malvinas” signals a position.
- Quotation target: Q-001, the Argentine slogan “Las Malvinas son argentinas.”
- Claims: C-001 on the war's unusual status since 1945; C-002 on the islands' population and 1833
  occupation; C-003 on the place of the Malvinas in Argentine national education.

## 2. Galtieri, the dictatorship, and the invasion gamble

- Transcript: U-00120–U-00232; original audio 07:27–14:30.
- Argument: a collapsing dictatorship chose invasion to manufacture legitimacy, betting on popular
  enthusiasm and British/American acquiescence.
- Narrative: economic crisis, desaparecidos, the replacement of Viola by Galtieri, Galtieri's US
  ties, and the December 1981 decision lead to the two wagers.
- Analogy: Halifax's treatment of Nazi Germany as an anti-communist bulwark.
- Quotations: Q-002, the Economist's alleged School of the Americas anecdote; Q-003, US praise of
  Galtieri and the regime.
- Claims: C-004 on the junta's crisis; C-005 on Galtieri's rise and US training; C-006 on the timing
  and rationale of the invasion decision.

## 3. Failed negotiations and Britain's blind spot

- Transcript: U-00233–U-00365; original audio 14:40–23:23.

- Quotations: Q-004, the February communiqué; Q-005, the 2 March Argentine statement; Q-006,

## 4. South Georgia and the escalation of March 1982

- Transcript: U-00366–U-00457; original audio 23:27–29:38.

- Digression: Astiz's later prosecution and the Carrington bus anecdote.
- Quotations: Q-007, Carrington's “the bus would not dare” anecdote; Q-008, Thatcher's memoir phrase

## 7. Invasion, Cabinet, and national humiliation

- Transcript: U-00622–U-00712; original audio 40:20–45:59.

## 9. Defeat transforms Argentina and Thatcher

- Transcript: U-00865–U-00965; original audio 55:16–61:32.

- Quotations: Q-018, an Argentine politician on never seeing a serious country; Q-019, the
