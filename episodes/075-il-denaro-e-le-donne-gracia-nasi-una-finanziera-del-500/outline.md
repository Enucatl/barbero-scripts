# Structural outline

## Overview

Barbero reconstructs the life of Gracia Nasi through the movement of her family, names, faith, and capital across the sixteenth-century Mediterranean. The lecture contrasts Christian persecution with Ottoman toleration, follows the banking house from Iberia through Antwerp, Venice, and Ferrara to Constantinople, and presents Gracia as both an exceptional business leader and a protector of displaced Sephardic Jews.

## 1. Una donna, una famiglia, molti nomi

- **Utterances:** U-00001–U-00077
- **Original audio:** 04:45–08:37
- **Argument:** The story joins money, inheritance, persecution, migration, and female authority; Gracia's several names embody the family's movement between public Christianity and Jewish identity.
- **Examples:** Renaissance wealth in gold; Christian and Muslim attitudes toward Jews; Nasi as a prestigious biblical title.
- **Claims:** C-001 Christian intolerance and Ottoman toleration; C-002 meaning and prestige of the name Nasi.

## 2. Giuseppe Nasi and the Venetian view

- **Utterances:** U-00078–U-00239
- **Original audio:** 08:41–17:55
- **Argument:** Barbero introduces Gracia through her nephew and son-in-law Giuseppe, whose position at Selim II's court alarmed Venetian diplomats and exposed both commercial rivalry and antisemitic conspiracy thinking.
- **Examples:** Marcantonio Barbaro's dispatches; Selim's drinking; the wine monopoly; Jewish intermediaries in Ottoman trade.
- **Quotations:** Q-001 Barbaro on Joseph's current name; Q-002 Barbaro on Selim and Miques; Q-003 Venetian complaints about Jewish “insolence.”
- **Claims:** C-003 Joseph's influence and wine business; C-004 Venetian-Jewish commercial rivalry.

## 3. From Spain to Portugal

- **Utterances:** U-00240–U-00388
- **Original audio:** 18:03–26:23
- **Argument:** The family's background begins with the 1492 expulsion, refuge and forced conversion in Portugal, and the double identity expressed by Beatriz de Luna and Gracia Nasi.
- **Examples:** Reconquista; Sephardic migration; limpieza de sangre; conversos and marranos.
- **Quotations:** Q-004 attributed remark of Bayezid II about Spain's king.
- **Claims:** C-005 the 1492 expulsion and Ottoman destinations; C-006 Portuguese conversion decree; C-007 genetic ancestry estimate.

## 4. Marriage, spices, and inheritance

- **Utterances:** U-00389–U-00484
- **Original audio:** 26:28–31:51
- **Argument:** Gracia marries Francisco Mendes within the crypto-Jewish kin network, participates in the spice fortune, and at twenty-five receives unusual authority over the banking house.
- **Examples:** secret religious observance; Portuguese spice routes; cousin marriage dispensations; Antwerp as Atlantic entrepôt.
- **Claims:** C-008 Francisco's testamentary appointment; C-009 Antwerp's commercial role.

## 5. The Inquisition and Antwerp

- **Utterances:** U-00485–U-00600
- **Original audio:** 31:56–38:17
- **Argument:** The Portuguese Inquisition drives Gracia to Antwerp, where intertwined marriages and deaths leave the sisters joint owners but Gracia sole administrator of the family capital.
- **Examples:** royal versus Roman inquisitions; Diogo and Brianna's marriage; mirrored daughters and names; flight routes through Venice.
- **Claims:** C-010 establishment of the Portuguese Inquisition; C-011 Diogo's testament and the sisters' unequal control.

## 6. Rescue network and imperial finance

- **Utterances:** U-00601–U-00684
- **Original audio:** 38:22–42:52
- **Argument:** Gracia's bank helps conversos move themselves and capital toward Ottoman safety, while a vast interest-free loan protects the family temporarily from Charles V before their covert departure.
- **Examples:** bills of exchange; the investigation for crypto-Judaism; the 100,000-ducat loan; the Aachen holiday pretext.
- **Claims:** C-012 Gracia's migration network; C-013 the loan to Charles V; C-014 the 1544 escape.

## 7. Venice and the disputed fortune

- **Utterances:** U-00685–U-00760
- **Original audio:** 42:55–47:24
- **Argument:** Venice offers relative safety but confines openly Jewish residents; Brianna's lawsuit lets the republic hold half the immense family capital while marriage schemes gather around the heiresses.
- **Examples:** the 1516 ghetto; Giudici del Forestier; Beatriz's deferred inheritance; Don Francisco of Aragon.
- **Claims:** C-015 Venetian ghetto rules; C-016 judgment over the sisters' capital.

## 8. Expulsion and refuge at Ferrara

- **Utterances:** U-00761–U-00876
- **Original audio:** 47:25–53:34
- **Argument:** Venice's expulsion of marranos pushes the family to tolerant Ferrara, where Gracia publicly resumes Judaism, funds Sephardic culture, and plans a definitive Ottoman move as ducal protection weakens.
- **Examples:** Renée of France and Calvin; Ferrara Bible; Samuel Usque's Consolation.
- **Quotations:** Q-005 Usque's praise of Gracia as the heart of the Portuguese nation.
- **Claims:** C-017 the 1550 expulsion; C-018 Gracia's patronage in Ferrara.

## 9. Departure for Constantinople

- **Utterances:** U-00877–U-00969
- **Original audio:** 53:41–59:15
- **Argument:** Gracia negotiates through the sultan to export the family and most of its assets; abduction, annulment, remarriage, and inheritance ultimately reunite both family and capital in Constantinople.
- **Examples:** Ottoman-Venetian negotiations; the 100,000-ducat deposit; Joseph's abduction of Beatriz; the cousins' later marriages.
- **Claims:** C-019 interstate negotiation over the fortune; C-020 final consolidation in Constantinople.

## 10. The lady of the Ottoman Empire

- **Utterances:** U-00970–U-00995
- **Original audio:** 59:17–60:34
- **Argument:** The Nasi house becomes a leading Ottoman financial firm while Gracia supports refugees and endows Jewish religious, medical, and educational institutions.
- **Claims:** C-021 Ottoman dependence on immigrant financial expertise; C-022 Gracia's charitable patronage.

## 11. Ancona and the boycott

- **Utterances:** U-00996–U-01046
- **Original audio:** 60:44–63:33
- **Argument:** Paul IV's persecution and execution of Ancona conversos prompt Gracia first to seek Ottoman diplomacy and then to organize an unprecedented international Jewish commercial boycott.
- **Claims:** C-023 the 1555–56 Ancona persecution; C-024 the attempted boycott.

## 12. Tiberias and the legacy

- **Utterances:** U-01047–U-01115
- **Original audio:** 63:36–67:09
- **Argument:** Gracia leases Tiberias to resettle Jewish refugees, a project later associated with Joseph; after her death he continues the family business through the era of Lepanto.
- **Quotations:** Q-006 French ambassador on Joseph seeking kingship of the Jews.
- **Claims:** C-025 the Tiberias project; C-026 Gracia's death and Joseph's Cypriot wine monopoly.
