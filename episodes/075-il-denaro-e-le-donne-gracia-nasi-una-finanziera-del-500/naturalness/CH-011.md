## 11. The burnings at Ancona and the boycott

<!-- chapter: CH-011; transcript: U-00996–U-01046 -->

Then something terrible happens. Hard as it may be to believe, Ancona has a fairly prosperous Jewish community, right there in the Papal States. The pope pushes antisemitic policies more aggressively than anyone. But these Jewish merchants and financiers are so valuable that, for many years, the popes allow Jews who have never converted to live openly in Ancona. Alongside them are many converts. The Jews are watched and confined to the ghetto, but the pope himself keeps the Inquisition from harassing them.

But times change. The age moves on, always in the same direction. In 1555, the pope dies, and Cardinal Carafa, the Inquisition’s leading champion, becomes Pope Paul IV. He backs it all the way and orders every converso in Ancona arrested. These are former Jews from Spain and Portugal who have presented themselves as Christians, though the Inquisition is convinced they’re lying. It arrests all of them.

Doña Gracia appeals to the sultan. And think about the kind of world this is. The pope and the sultan officially hate each other. Officially, they don’t even have contact. In practice, their representatives meet and talk, and the two sides put pressure on each other. At Gracia’s urging, the sultan contacts the pope on behalf of the prisoners. He asks that they be released and their property returned.

The negotiations drag on. Eventually, the pope agrees to return the money and lift the seizure of the prisoners’ property, but he refuses to free them. People who pretend to become Christians while remaining Jewish have to be made an example of. In 1556, twenty-four Jews are burned at the stake in Ancona. Among them is the local agent for Gracia Nasi’s firm.

Gracia responds by reaching out to the entire international Jewish business community. She proposes a boycott of Ancona: no more business with the pope. The boycott proves hard to carry out because, in the end, business is business. But the fact that she can even imagine organizing something like this tells you who she is.
