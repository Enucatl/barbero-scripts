## 12. Tiberias, death, and the legacy

<!-- chapter: CH-012; transcript: U-01047–U-01115 -->

She was a person who, a few years later, in 1558—now we are nearing the end, and Gracia is growing old, but we must still tell this story—had an idea. The sultan was Süleyman the Magnificent, not yet his son Selim, the alcoholic with whom we began. In 1558 Gracia discussed her idea with Süleyman and obtained his consent.

She leased a piece of the Holy Land: the region of Tiberias in Galilee, containing the ruins of the ancient city and many surrounding villages. Jews had not lived there since time immemorial, but Gracia’s idea was to bring them back to the Holy Land. With the sultan’s consent she leased Tiberias, built a settlement, and began transferring refugees arriving from the Christian world. In her own way, in short, she had also invented Zionism: the idea of returning the Jews to the Holy Land.

The settlement functioned for a time, then did not last long. The Jews fleeing Europe were almost all merchants and financiers. What were they to do there in the Palestinian desert? The undertaking did not prosper, though the project was still discussed a few years later.

By then it was attributed to the nephew Joseph, who, as we have seen, was a kind of bogeyman to Westerners and Christians. Whenever he was involved, they feared the worst. A French ambassador in Constantinople wrote that the Jew Miques, Joseph Nasi, had again obtained the sultan’s permission to build a city on Lake Tiberias where only Jews could live and that, as far as could be judged, he planned to make himself king of the Jews. Once again, you see what these people represented in the political imagination of the Christian world.

Every life eventually ends. In 1569 Doña Gracia fell ill and died, leaving the baton to her nephew. For years he continued to manage the company and passed through difficult times. The defeat at Lepanto has gone down in Christian history as the moment when Christendom triumphed over the Muslim world, the Ottoman Empire. In reality it was certainly a grand and disastrous battle, but one that literally left matters as they were, because the Ottomans ultimately won that war and obtained what they wanted: Cyprus.

Joseph Nasi naturally obtained the monopoly on wine exports from Cyprus. Thus, as if the capital accumulated by his aunt had not been enough, the family continued to accumulate more.

It would be nice if I had a brilliant conclusion, but in truth I do not. That was the story. Gracia died; that world ended and is far away. So many things have been overturned, including arguments about tolerance and the capacity to be tolerant. But in any case, I hope it seemed a good story to you. Thank you.
