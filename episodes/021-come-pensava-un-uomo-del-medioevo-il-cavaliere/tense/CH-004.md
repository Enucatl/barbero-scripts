## 4. Meat on Friday: intention versus observance

<!-- chapter: CH-004; transcript: U-00158–U-00197 -->

Joinville experiences this himself near the disastrous end of the crusade. The Turks have won,
massacred half the crusader army, and captured everyone else. Joinville describes these scenes with
extraordinary power: collapse, flight, chaos, surrender. And surrender is a gamble. You don't know
whose hands you'll fall into or what the Saracen Turks will do. Some see prisoners who are wounded
or sick, strike them on the head, and throw them into the sea. Others treat their prisoners very
courteously. The king and great lords receive courteous treatment, partly because they can pay large
ransoms. Christian and Muslim kings and lords understand one another; they belong to the same world
and have a great deal in common, as we'll see in a moment.

So things are already more or less over. Joinville is a prisoner and is having lunch with an emir
who has invited him to eat. The emir has invited him to lunch, and they are chatting through an
interpreter, naturally. They are happily discussing their respective family trees and mutual
acquaintances, because the emir is very interested in Emperor Frederick II. It emerges that
Joinville is related to Emperor Frederick II, and there they are, comparing acquaintances and
ancestors as they eat.

Joinville’s account is more precise: While we were eating, he had a citizen of Paris brought before
us. When the citizen arrived, he said to me, 'My lord, what are you doing?' 'Why, what am I doing?'
I said. 'In God's name,' he said, 'you are eating meat on a Friday!' When I heard this, I pushed my
bowl away. The admiral asked my Saracen why I had done that, and he told him; the admiral replied
that God would not hold it against me, since I had not done it knowingly. And you should know that
the legate gave me the same answer once we were out of prison. Yet that did not stop me from fasting
afterward on bread and water every Friday in Lent. The legate became very angry with me about this,
because I was the only great lord who had remained with the king.
