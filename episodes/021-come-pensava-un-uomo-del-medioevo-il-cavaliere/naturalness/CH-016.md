## 16. Honor destroys the battle plan

<!-- chapter: CH-016; transcript: U-00676–U-00698 -->

That danger appears at Mansurah in 1250. During the attack, the Templars are assigned the vanguard,
the first position and a place of honor. Robert of Artois, the king's brother, leads another group.
As the enemy retreats, Robert's men press forward past the Templars. The order of the advance begins
to break down.

The Templars follow: they do not want to be seen hanging back while another division takes their
place. What begins as pursuit carries the advance into the city's streets, where the force becomes
trapped. Robert is killed, along with most of that Templar contingent. It is a devastating loss,
though the crusade's final collapse also comes from later shortages, illness, and the failed
withdrawal.

The episode shows how quickly the fear of dishonor can disrupt command. Yet that same concern for
honor can keep a fighter at his post when he is frightened. Joinville remembers another moment at
Mansurah when a fellow knight uses a joke about future glory to give courage to them both.
