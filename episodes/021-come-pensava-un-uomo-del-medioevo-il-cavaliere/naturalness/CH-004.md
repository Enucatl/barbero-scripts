## 4. Meat on Friday: intention versus observance

<!-- chapter: CH-004; transcript: U-00158–U-00197 -->

Joinville experiences this himself near the disastrous end of the crusade. The Egyptian forces have
defeated the crusader army, and Joinville describes the retreat and capture with extraordinary
power: collapse, flight, chaos, surrender. Surrender is a gamble. Some captors kill prisoners who
are wounded or sick; others spare them. A noble prisoner may be valuable for ransom, but his rank
does not guarantee his safety.

Joinville is now a prisoner. The chief admiral of the Egyptian galleys has summoned him and asked
about his claimed kinship with Emperor Frederick II. They are eating when another captive, a man
from Paris, notices something Joinville has forgotten amid the battle and the confusion.

Joinville’s account is more precise: While we were eating, he had a citizen of Paris brought before
us. When the citizen arrived, he said to me, 'My lord, what are you doing?' 'Why, what am I doing?'
I said. 'In God's name,' he said, 'you are eating meat on a Friday!' When I heard this, I pushed my
bowl away. The admiral asked my Saracen why I had done that, and he told him; the admiral replied
that God would not hold it against me, since I had not done it knowingly. And you should know that
the legate gave me the same answer once we were out of prison. Yet that did not stop me from fasting
afterward on bread and water every Friday in Lent. The legate became very angry with me about this,
because I was the only great lord who had remained with the king.

He has lost track of the day and eaten meat on a Friday. His interpreter explains the sudden
reaction to the Muslim official, who says God will not hold an unknowing act against him. Later the
papal legate says the same. Joinville still fasts on bread and water every Friday in Lent afterward.
The legate is angry, but Joinville cannot let it go: for him, observing the rule matters even though
the breach was accidental.
