## 14. Revolution, Terror, and the tax farmers' reckoning

<!-- chapter: CH-014; transcript: U-01090–U-01116 -->

For a while, the king goes along with the new arrangement. But deep down, he can't stand the humiliation. He starts plotting with foreign powers to invade France. The plot is uncovered, and the king and queen are arrested.

The tension is overwhelming. People are terrified. Heads begin to fall, and the French Revolution enters the Terror.

One last thing before we finish. Amid all these beheadings, trial follows trial. Anyone accused of plotting with the king to overthrow the new order, restore the absolute monarchy, or profit from the old system is hauled into court.

The mood is simple: now the profiteers will answer for what they've done. If there isn't a penny left, it's because they all had their snouts in the trough. Now the people will judge them.

Then one day, all the surviving *fermiers généraux*—the financiers who ran tax collection under the monarchy—are put on trial together. On May 8, 1794, twenty-eight former *fermiers généraux* go to the guillotine together.

There's much more we could say, but by now I think the larger meaning of this story is clear to everyone. Thank you. Thank you all, have a good evening—and I'll see you next year.
