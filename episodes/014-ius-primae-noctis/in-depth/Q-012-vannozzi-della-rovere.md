# Q-012: Vannozzi and Cardinal della Rovere

## Research question

At U-00795–U-00803, Barbero says that an early-seventeenth-century scholar reported a personal
conversation with Cardinal Gerolamo della Rovere. The cardinal allegedly said that his family had
possessed a written privilege granting a first-night right and that he had burned the parchment.

The source was identified through Alain Boureau's bibliography as Bonifacio Vannozzi,
*Della suppellettile degli avvertimenti politici, morali e christiani*, volume 2 (1610), printed
page 253.

## Edition and pagination

The relevant witness is volume 2, published in Bologna in 1610 by the heirs of Giovanni Rossi. It
must not be confused with the separately digitized 1609 volume 1. The passage occurs on printed
page 253 immediately before avvertimento 530.

## Exact ledger excerpt

> Cotal costume, da Pagani, & da Gentili, fù già in Piemonte, & il Cardinale Illustrissimo
> Hieronimo della Rovere mi diceva, haver egli stesso, abbruciato il privilegio, che havea di ciò la
> sua Casa.

Direct English translation:

> Such a custom formerly existed in Piedmont among pagans and gentiles, and the Most Illustrious
> Cardinal Hieronimo della Rovere told me that he himself had burned the privilege that his house
> possessed concerning it.

The supplied OCR initially read `Illustrassimo`; visual comparison with the page image confirmed
the printed form `Illustrissimo`, which is used here and in the ledger.

## What “the privilege” means in context

The antecedent of *di ciò* is not merely a wedding payment. Vannozzi first describes the legendary
Scottish law requiring a bride to give the flower of her virginity to the local lord, followed by
Malcolm's supposed substitution of a gold payment. He then says the same custom formerly existed
in Piedmont. In the following sentence he explicitly describes sovereign lords issuing written
privileges that allowed subordinate lords to deflower other men's brides.

Vannozzi therefore interprets the Della Rovere document as an expressly sexual privilege. The
possibility raised orally by Barbero—that the destroyed document may really have concerned a
marriage payment such as *culagium*—is Barbero's skeptical historical inference, not an ambiguity
expressed by Vannozzi.

## Evidentiary assessment

The passage confirms all essential elements of Barbero's attribution:

- The scholar is Bonifacio Vannozzi.
- The work appeared at the beginning of the seventeenth century.
- Vannozzi presents the report as a personal conversation: *mi diceva*.
- The named speaker is Cardinal Hieronimo della Rovere.
- The cardinal allegedly said that he personally burned the privilege held by his family.
- Vannozzi understood the privilege as authorizing sexual access to brides.

It does not prove that such a charter actually existed or that the Della Rovere family historically
exercised such a custom. It is a 1610 report of the cardinal's retrospective claim, embedded in a
moralizing discussion that accepts legendary material about Malcolm of Scotland. The quotation is
confirmed; the underlying medieval institution is not.
