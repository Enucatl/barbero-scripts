The *ius primae noctis* is an extraordinary fantasy that the Middle Ages creates, which arises at the end of the Middle Ages, and in which people believe so strongly that there is almost a risk that someone will want to put it into practice for real, although there is no indication that this ever really happens. In reality it is a fantasy; it never exists. Does that mean the lord did not sometimes sleep with the village girl? No, it does not mean that. The lord sleeps with the village girl, by fair means or foul. These are things that happen in every unequal society. But there is an immense difference between a society in which it happens that the rich man takes advantage and sleeps with the poor girl, and imagining instead—which is true madness—a society where this is a right to which everyone must resign themselves because the law says so. To say that the lord slept with the village girls is like saying that in the nineteenth century—that in the nineteenth century the owner of the spinning mill or his son sometimes sleeps with a female worker. It is as if the owner of the spinning mill sometimes slept with the female worker. There are a thousand reasons why this can happen, but obviously no one dreams of saying it is a right. To speak of it as a law means wanting to invent a past. And so I would close with a passage that I find extraordinary, which speaks precisely of the invention of the past. It is taken from Orwell's novel *1984*. At one point in Orwell's novel it discusses how, under Big Brother's regime, the past is reinvented for the people. In the past, the people are taught, there are evil creatures called capitalists, and the capitalists oppress the people in every way, with every kind of abuse and oppression. I quote verbatim: there is also something called the *ius primae noctis*, which probably ought not to be mentioned in schoolbooks for children. It was the law by which every capitalist had the right to sleep with any woman working in one of his factories. There. I find this fantastic because Orwell has identified the mechanism exactly. Something that can happen in reality, and is an act of violence and an effect of social relations, is imagined to have been a law. Because imagining a society in which such a thing could be a law means imagining a society truly alien and totally barbaric in relation to our values. And so, precisely, when we want to rewrite the past and imagine it, and be pleased that we live in a much better world than the past, the *ius primae noctis* is perfect for furnishing that past. Thank you.
