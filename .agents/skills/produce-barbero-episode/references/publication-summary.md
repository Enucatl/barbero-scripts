# Publication summaries

Read the final English recording script and write `publication.summary` in `episode.yaml`.
Use this prompt when creating or regenerating a summary:

> Write a brief invitation to listen, with enough context to orient the reader and something
> still worth discovering. Find an intriguing detail in the script and let it work: an awkward
> moment, an unlikely confidence, a puzzling fact, or an unfinished action can carry the appeal.
> Leave room for suspense instead of explaining the episode's argument and outcome. A familiar
> ending need not be hidden; curiosity can lie in how anyone arrived there. Keep the language
> natural and lightly told, using idiomatic American English. Avoid a lecturing tone, abstract
> explanations of historical meaning, and generic "Explore", "Discover", or "Follow" invitations.
>
> Avoid the "list effect": no "item, item, item" catalogue of people, events, themes, or
> anecdotes, including lists disguised as prose. Do not compress the chapter outline into a
> paragraph. Ground every claim in the script and preserve uncertainty. Suspense must come from
> the material, without invented danger, misleading omissions, or clickbait.
>
> Give each episode its own voice and shape. Let some be wry, others intimate or unsettling,
> as the subject warrants. Vary length, sentence rhythm, openings, and endings across the set;
> don't keep repeating a protagonist, a contrast, and an explanation. Questions, clipped
> sentences, and ominous last lines are options, not a template. Sometimes two sentences are
> enough; sometimes a short scene needs more room. Read neighboring summaries together and
> rewrite any that sound as if they came off the same production line.

Episode 019's appeal comes from the friar's vivid personality, without a catalogue of his
interests. Preserve that liveliness without imposing its structure or tone on every episode.
Read the result aloud and check it against the script before saving it. For summary-only revisions,
preserve the other metadata fields.
