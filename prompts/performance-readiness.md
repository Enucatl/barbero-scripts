# Recording and performance readiness

Prepare `{episode_directory}` for a complete timed read-through. Read the corrected transcript,
outline, research ledgers, annotated English script, and recording script in full.

## Spoken-English pass

- Preserve Barbero's sequence, interest, purposeful repetition, questions, delayed reveals,
  analogies, and digressions.
- Replace grammatical but translated-sounding English with idiomatic conversation.
- Use natural contractions unless a full form provides emphasis.
- Add familiar idioms only occasionally, at genuine oral beats; avoid decorative slang or a voice
  foreign to the rest of the lecture.
- Fix unclear referents, awkward breaths, tongue-twisting clauses, accidental repetition, and
  transitions that sound written rather than spoken.
- Do not compress for a target word count or rewrite the lecture as a documentary summary.
- Never alter verified quotation wording for fluency. Preserve qualifications required by the
  ledgers and keep the annotated and recording copies synchronized through `render`.

## Pronunciation and continuity

Build or update the outline's pronunciation-target list from names and source-language phrases
actually present in the English script. Add unobtrusive pronunciation cues to the annotated script
only where the narrator needs them; ensure rendering retains intended cues and removes research
markers.

Check every section against its transcript range for omissions, duplicated events, chronology,
and narrative handoffs. Confirm that every script marker resolves, every ledger treatment is
honored, anecdotal material is labelled, and the recording copy contains no research markers or
draft comments.

## Timed-read handoff

Report the recording word count and estimated durations at 150, 160, and 170 words per minute.
Create no fictional timing result: the narrator must supply the actual duration after reading the
complete script aloud. During that read, record section, phrase, issue type, and proposed fix for
every stumble, breath problem, pronunciation question, pacing problem, or passage that sounds
written.

After human read-through notes are supplied, apply only approved performance edits, render again,
run formatting, tests, and editorial validation, update outline counts/statuses, and report any
remaining risk. Do not create a Git commit.
